Do You Qualify for GLP-1 Weight Loss? BMI & Eligibility Explained

The first question almost every patient asks is the same one: Do I even qualify? It's a reasonable place to start — GLP-1 medications are among the most effective weight-loss tools available, but they are prescription treatments, not supplements, and they carry real eligibility criteria, real contraindications, and real considerations about cost and long-term commitment. This guide walks through exactly what the FDA has approved, what the clinical trial data shows for outcomes, and what the process looks like in Utah.

The FDA Eligibility Criteria: Who Qualifies for GLP-1 Weight Loss Medications?

Both FDA-approved GLP-1 weight loss medications — Wegovy (semaglutide 2.4 mg) and Zepbound (tirzepatide) — share the same fundamental eligibility framework: a BMI of 30 or higher (obesity), or a BMI of 27 or higher (overweight) with at least one qualifying weight-related health condition. If you have a BMI of 31, you meet the primary criterion outright — no comorbidity required.

Both drugs are approved as adjuncts to a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity. They require a medical evaluation and an ongoing prescription relationship. This is not a one-time visit; these are treatments for a chronic condition.

Qualifying Weight-Related Health Conditions at BMI 27–29

If your BMI is between 27 and 29.9, at least one of the following conditions must be present and documented:

  • Hypertension (high blood pressure)
  • Type 2 diabetes
  • Dyslipidemia (elevated LDL cholesterol or triglycerides, or low HDL)
  • Obstructive sleep apnea
  • Established cardiovascular disease — listed as a qualifying comorbidity in Zepbound's label, and covered independently by a 2024 FDA indication update for Wegovy (see below)

One clarification worth making: prediabetes (HbA1c 5.7–6.4%) is not explicitly listed as a qualifying weight-related comorbidity in the current weight management labeling for either medication, even though GLP-1 therapy has been shown to reduce conversion from prediabetes to type 2 diabetes substantially. If prediabetes is your primary or only comorbidity and your BMI is under 30, discuss the specific prescribing rationale with your provider.

The 2024 Cardiovascular Indication: A Separate Pathway

On March 8, 2024, the FDA granted Wegovy an additional indication: reducing cardiovascular death, heart attack, and stroke risk in adults with established cardiovascular disease (prior MI, prior stroke, or peripheral artery disease) who have overweight or obesity. This was based on the SELECT trial — 17,604 patients, roughly 20% relative risk reduction in major cardiovascular events. This indication opened Medicare coverage for a subset of patients; it also expanded the clinical rationale for prescribing Wegovy to patients whose BMI might be only modestly elevated but whose cardiovascular risk is significant.

Who Should NOT Take GLP-1 Medications?

GLP-1 medications carry an FDA boxed warning — the most serious category — and are contraindicated in specific populations. A thorough medical history is not optional; it's the foundation of a safe prescribing decision.

Absolute Contraindications (FDA Boxed Warning)

  • Personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) — GLP-1 receptor agonists caused thyroid C-cell tumors in rodent studies; the human relevance is uncertain, but the FDA requires contraindication in this population
  • Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN2) — associated with elevated MTC risk; absolute contraindication for both drugs
  • Pregnancy — discontinue at least 2 months before a planned pregnancy; fetal harm risk is documented in animal studies
  • Known hypersensitivity to semaglutide, tirzepatide, or any formulation excipient

Important Precautions — Not Absolute Contraindications, but Requiring Careful Assessment

  • History of pancreatitis — GLP-1 agents are associated with increased pancreatitis risk; use with caution and discontinue immediately if symptoms arise
  • Gallbladder disease — rapid weight loss of any cause increases gallstone risk; GLP-1 use appears to compound this
  • Diabetic retinopathy — patients with T2DM and rapid glycemic improvement have shown worsening retinopathy in some studies; ophthalmologic monitoring recommended
  • Acute kidney injury risk — primarily from dehydration secondary to GI side effects (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea); hydration guidance matters during dose escalation
  • Suicidal ideation/behavior — FDA labeling for both drugs includes a monitoring requirement; reported in post-marketing surveillance, causal relationship not established

None of these precautions is a reason to automatically avoid GLP-1 therapy — but each requires a direct, informed conversation with your prescribing provider before starting.

What the Clinical Trials Actually Show for Weight Loss

These are not drugs with marginal or uncertain effects. The pivotal clinical trials produced weight loss outcomes that are genuinely unprecedented in the pharmacological history of obesity treatment.

Semaglutide (Wegovy) — STEP-1 Trial

The STEP-1 trial (Wilding et al., NEJM 2021) enrolled 1,961 adults with BMI ≥30, or ≥27 with a qualifying comorbidity, without type 2 diabetes. At 68 weeks:

  • Mean body weight reduction: –14.9% (semaglutide) vs. –2.4% (placebo)
  • ≥5% body weight loss: 86.4% of participants on semaglutide
  • ≥10% body weight loss: 69.1%
  • ≥15% body weight loss: 50.5% — more than half of participants

For context: previous best-in-class pharmacotherapy achieved roughly 5–10% mean weight loss at best. A 15% mean weight reduction with half of trial participants exceeding that threshold was a meaningful shift in what obesity pharmacotherapy could achieve.

Tirzepatide (Zepbound) — SURMOUNT-1 Trial

The SURMOUNT-1 trial (Jastreboff et al., NEJM 2022) enrolled 2,539 adults with BMI ≥30, or ≥27 with a qualifying condition, without type 2 diabetes. At 72 weeks:

  • Tirzepatide 15 mg: mean weight reduction of –20.9%
  • Tirzepatide 10 mg: –21.4%
  • Tirzepatide 5 mg: –16.0%
  • Placebo: –3.1%

Approximately 90% of tirzepatide participants lost weight. Fat mass reduction in the 15 mg group averaged 33.9%. The ADA's 2025 Standards of Care classified both tirzepatide and injectable semaglutide as "very-high-efficacy" weight-loss agents. A 2026 head-to-head trial (SURMOUNT-5) found tirzepatide outperforming semaglutide at 72 weeks (–20.2% vs. –13.7%), with 81.6% of tirzepatide patients achieving ≥10% weight loss compared to 31.6% on semaglutide.

One Crucial Reality: What Happens When You Stop

The ADA and clinical researchers are consistent on this point: these medications treat a chronic biological condition, not a short-term deficit of willpower. When patients discontinue GLP-1 therapy, they typically regain roughly half to two-thirds of their lost weight within one year, along with reversal of the metabolic improvements achieved. This makes the program design surrounding the medication — the nutritional guidance, the exercise habits, the follow-up structure — every bit as important as the prescription itself. A medication that succeeds temporarily isn't the same as a program that produces lasting change.

What About Compounded Semaglutide — Is That Still an Option in 2026?

The short answer is no — not through legitimate pharmacy channels, and not safely through foreign sources.

During 2023–2024, widespread drug shortages for both semaglutide and tirzepatide allowed compounding pharmacies to legally prepare these medications under FDA shortage provisions. That window is closed. The FDA declared the semaglutide shortage officially resolved on February 21, 2025, and tirzepatide's shortage was resolved on December 19, 2024. Deadlines for compounding pharmacies (503A) and outsourcing facilities (503B) to stop manufacturing these drugs passed in April–May 2025. In April 2026, the FDA proposed permanently removing both drugs from the 503B bulk compounding list.

As of mid-2026, the FDA had received more than 455 adverse event reports from patients using compounded semaglutide and more than 320 from compounded tirzepatide. Patients encountering online vendors still offering "compounded semaglutide" at steep discounts should approach this with significant caution — the legal compounding period has ended, and product quality and purity outside the regulated channel cannot be verified.

The brand-name manufacturers now offer meaningful cash-pay programs. Novo Nordisk's direct program offers Wegovy at $499/month; Eli Lilly's LillyDirect vial program for Zepbound runs $299–$449/month depending on dose. These are substantial reductions from list prices ($1,350 and $1,059/month, respectively) and represent the legitimate lower-cost access path that was previously filled by compounding.

Does Insurance Cover GLP-1 Weight Loss Medications?

Coverage is improving but remains inconsistent — and navigating it requires knowing which indication your insurer covers.

Among large employers (5,000+ workers), 43% now cover GLP-1 medications for weight loss in 2025, up from 28% in 2024, according to KFF survey data. However, more than 88% of commercial plans that do cover these drugs require prior authorization. ACA Marketplace plans cover Wegovy for the obesity indication in less than 1% of plans, though Ozempic (the diabetes formulation) is covered in 82%.

Medicare Part D covers Wegovy specifically for the cardiovascular risk reduction indication — patients with established cardiovascular disease plus overweight or obesity. This covers an estimated 3.6 million Medicare beneficiaries. Medicare does not cover GLP-1 medications for obesity alone. Following CMS price negotiation in late 2025, the negotiated Medicare price for semaglutide is approximately $274/month, effective for 2026 enrollees — a significant reduction from list price.

Utah's Medicaid program (CHIP/Medicaid) coverage varies; a prior authorization process is standard for any public-payer coverage of these medications.

Frequently Asked Questions

What BMI qualifies you for GLP-1 weight loss medications?

A BMI of 27–29.9 qualifies if you also have at least one weight-related health condition: hypertension, type 2 diabetes, dyslipidemia, obstructive sleep apnea, or established cardiovascular disease.

Does a BMI of 31 qualify for tirzepatide?

Yes. A BMI of 31 meets the primary obesity criterion (BMI ≥30) for Zepbound (tirzepatide) for chronic weight management. A medical evaluation is required to rule out contraindications and confirm the treatment is appropriate for you individually.

What conditions qualify you for weight loss injections at BMI 27–29?

FDA-approved qualifying conditions at BMI 27–29.9 include: high blood pressure, type 2 diabetes, dyslipidemia (cholesterol/triglyceride abnormalities), obstructive sleep apnea, and (for Zepbound) established cardiovascular disease. Prediabetes is not explicitly listed as a qualifying comorbidity in the weight management labeling.

Can I get Ozempic for weight loss if I don't have diabetes?

Prescribing Ozempic off-label for weight loss in non-diabetic patients is legal but represents an off-label use with a different dosing protocol than the weight-management trials used.

The Bottom Line

If your BMI is 30 or above, you meet the FDA's primary eligibility threshold for both Wegovy and Zepbound — no additional health conditions required. At BMI 27–29.9, a qualifying condition (hypertension, type 2 diabetes, dyslipidemia, sleep apnea, or CVD) is needed. The clinical trial data for these medications is among the most compelling ever published for obesity pharmacotherapy — 15–21% average weight loss, with the majority of trial participants reaching clinically meaningful thresholds.

Eligibility, however, is not the same as appropriateness. Contraindications matter. Program structure matters. And for many patients — particularly men dealing with overlapping issues of weight, low testosterone, and metabolic fatigue — the conversation about GLP-1 therapy is part of a broader picture that includes hormonal health, muscle preservation, and cardiovascular function.

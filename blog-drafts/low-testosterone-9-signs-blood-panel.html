Could your fatigue, low sex drive, and stubborn belly fat be caused by low testosterone? Quite possibly. If you are a man over 30 noticing several of these changes at once, low testosterone (also called "low T" or hypogonadism) is a common and treatable cause — and the only way to know for certain is a simple blood panel. Below are nine signs that warrant testing, what a testosterone panel actually measures, and what current research says about treatment safety.

Testosterone naturally declines by roughly 1% per year after age 30, according to the Cleveland Clinic. By age 45, an estimated 40% of men have clinically low testosterone, and prevalence keeps climbing with each decade. You do not have to accept these symptoms as "just getting older."

What is low testosterone?

Low testosterone is a medical condition in which the body does not produce enough of the hormone that regulates a man's energy, muscle mass, mood, metabolism, and sexual function. The American Urological Association (AUA) generally defines testosterone deficiency as a total testosterone level below 300 ng/dL confirmed on two morning blood tests, combined with symptoms. Because symptoms overlap with other conditions, bloodwork — not guesswork — is essential for a diagnosis.

9 signs you should get a testosterone blood panel

1. Persistent fatigue and low energy

Feeling drained even after a full night's sleep is one of the most common signs of low T. If your usual coffee and rest no longer restore your energy, your hormones may be a factor.

2. Reduced sex drive (low libido)

Testosterone is the primary driver of male libido. A noticeable, ongoing drop in interest in sex — not tied to stress or relationship issues — is a classic red flag worth testing.

3. Erectile difficulties

While erectile dysfunction has many causes (often vascular), low testosterone can contribute. When ED appears alongside low libido and fatigue, a hormone panel helps identify whether low T is part of the picture.

4. Loss of muscle mass and strength

Testosterone helps build and maintain muscle. Men with low T often notice their workouts produce fewer results and that strength or muscle tone is slipping despite consistent effort.

5. Increased body fat, especially around the midsection

Low testosterone is linked to gains in body fat — particularly abdominal fat — and can make weight loss frustratingly difficult. Fat tissue also converts testosterone to estrogen, which can worsen the cycle.

6. Mood changes, irritability, or low motivation

Hormones influence mood. Men with low T frequently report irritability, a flat or "blah" mood, reduced drive and confidence, and in some cases symptoms of depression.

7. Brain fog and trouble concentrating

Difficulty focusing, memory lapses, and a general sense of mental fog are commonly reported by men with low testosterone and often improve once levels are optimized.

8. Poor or disrupted sleep

Low T is associated with insomnia and reduced sleep quality. Sleep and testosterone are deeply connected — poor sleep lowers testosterone, and low testosterone worsens sleep.

9. Other physical changes

Reduced body or facial hair, decreased bone density, hot flashes, or a drop in overall vitality can also signal low testosterone, especially when several appear together.

When should you get a blood panel?

If you recognize two or more of the signs above and they have persisted for several weeks, it is reasonable to get tested — regardless of your age. Notably, low T is no longer just an "older man" issue: one study found a 58% increase in men aged 35–44 starting testosterone therapy between 2018 and 2022. Testosterone is best measured in the morning (typically before 10 a.m.), when levels peak, and a diagnosis requires two separate confirming tests.

What a testosterone blood panel measures

A thorough hormone panel looks at far more than a single number. At Mint Medical Clinic, evaluation is based on a comprehensive blood panel that typically includes:

  • Total testosterone — the overall amount in your blood.
  • Free testosterone — the portion actually available for your body to use.
  • SHBG (sex hormone-binding globulin) — affects how much testosterone is usable.
  • LH and FSH — pituitary signals that help pinpoint the cause.
  • Estradiol — estrogen levels, important for balance and side-effect management.

Looking at these markers together — rather than total testosterone alone — is what allows for a truly personalized treatment plan.

Is testosterone therapy safe? What the 2025 FDA update changed

For years, testosterone products carried a cardiovascular "black box" warning. In February 2025, following the large TRAVERSE safety trial, the U.S. Food & Drug Administration removed that warning and replaced it with updated, more accurate labeling. This reflects a growing body of evidence on the safety of properly supervised testosterone replacement therapy (TRT). As always, TRT should be medically supervised, individualized, and monitored with follow-up bloodwork — results and suitability vary from person to person.

Frequently asked questions

What are the first signs you need testosterone replacement therapy?

The earliest and most common signs are persistent fatigue, reduced sex drive, and difficulty building or maintaining muscle. When these appear together and last more than a few weeks, a morning blood panel can confirm whether low testosterone is the cause.

At what testosterone level should you start TRT?

The American Urological Association considers a total testosterone below 300 ng/dL — confirmed on two morning tests — alongside symptoms as the threshold for diagnosing testosterone deficiency. Treatment decisions are individualized and based on both your numbers and your symptoms.

Can low testosterone be fixed naturally?

Sleep, strength training, weight loss, stress reduction, and treating underlying conditions can meaningfully support healthy testosterone. For clinically low levels, these steps may not be enough on their own, which is why testing first is important to determine the right approach.

How long does it take for TRT to work?

Many men notice improvements in energy, mood, and libido within 3 to 6 weeks, while changes in muscle mass and body composition typically develop over several months. Ongoing monitoring helps fine-tune your plan.

Is low testosterone only a problem for older men?

No. While prevalence rises with age, low testosterone increasingly affects men in their 30s and 40s. Testing is appropriate at any age when symptoms are present.
